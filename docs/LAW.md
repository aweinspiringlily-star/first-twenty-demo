## California and San Jose obligations by headcount

- **a-wc-1** [confirmed, threshold 1] Every California employer must secure workers' compensation coverage, either an insurance policy or a state self-insurance certificate, even if it has only one employee (Labor Code 3700). Value: 1 employee. Effective: Ongoing. Counting: Any single employee, full- or part-time.. App: Blocking checklist task before the first hire: bind a workers' comp policy and store the carrier's name, address and phone in Settings. The LC 2810.5 notice and the SB 294 notice both need these details. Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dwc/employer.htm
  - Checker note: The DIR DWC employer page says California employers must have workers' compensation insurance 'even if they have only one employee.' Labor Code 3700 gives every employer except the state two ways to secure payment: (a) insurance from a carrier authorized in CA, or (b) a certificate of consent to self-insure from the Director of Industrial Relations. leginfo.legislature.ca.gov returned 403 to direct fetch, so the 3700 wording comes from search excerpts of the leginfo section page and DIR self-insurance rulemaking documents. Self-insurance is not realistic for a 1-20 person startup, so the app should default to the insured option.
- **a-edd-register-1** [confirmed, threshold 1] An employer must register with EDD for a payroll tax account (online through e-Services for Business, or on paper form DE 1) within 15 days of paying more than $100 in wages in a calendar quarter. Value: More than $100 in wages in a quarter; register within 15 days. Effective: Ongoing. Counting: Triggered by wages paid to any employee, not by headcount.. App: Compliance item due 15 days after the first payroll that pushes quarterly wages over $100. Store the EDD employer account number in Settings. Source: https://edd.ca.gov/en/payroll_taxes/step-1-register-as-an-employer/
  - Checker note: The EDD Step 1 page says 'You must register as an employer within 15 days' if you 'Pay more than $100 in wages in a calendar quarter'. The household-employer threshold is $750 and does not apply here. The DE 1 instructions (edd.ca.gov/siteassets/files/pdf_pub_ctr/de1i.pdf) give the statute as CUIC 1086: register 'within 15 days after hiring one or more employees and paying wages in excess of $100' in a calendar quarter. Registration methods: online through e-Services for Business, or a paper DE 1 by mail or fax. Paper processing can take up to 14 days.

Value: Within 20 days of the start date. Effective: Ongoing. Counting: Every new or rehired employee working in California.. App: Auto-created onboarding task due on start date + 20 calendar days.   - Checker note: The EDD New Hire Reporting page sets the report deadline at 20 calendar days from the start-of-work date, which it defines as 'the first day services were performed for wages'. A rehire is someone separated from the same employer for at least 60 consecutive days, after the employment relationship actually ended. The DE 34 'Report of New Employee(s)' can be filed through e-Services for Business or on paper by mail or fax.
- **a-psl-1** [confirmed, threshold 1] All California employers must provide at least 40 hours or 5 days (whichever is more) of paid sick leave per year (SB 616), either up front or accrued at 1 hour per 30 hours worked; employees may use it after 90 days of employment, and the employer may cap accrual at 80 hours or 10 days. Value: 40 hrs / 5 days per year; accrue 1 hr per 30 hrs; 90-day use wait; cap 80 hrs / 10 days. Effective: Since January 1, 2024 (40 hr / 5 day level); poster updated for 2026. Counting: Covers all employees, including part-time and temporary, who work at least 30 days within a year in California for the employer.. App: Settings validator for the PTO policy: an up-front grant must be at least 40 hrs / 5 days, and an accrual plan at least 1:30 with any cap of 80 hrs or more. Post the 2026 paid sick leave poster; per the Labor Commissioner's Dec 2025 letter, permitted uses now include jury duty, court witness duty and crime-victim situations. Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/paid_sick_leave.htm
  - Checker note: The Labor Commissioner FAQ (updated Dec 31, 2025) confirms all of the following. Amount: 5 days or 40 hours from Jan 1, 2024. Accrual: at least 1 hour per 30 hours worked. Accrual cap: 10 days or 80 hours, whichever is more. Coverage: employees who work at least 30 days within a year in CA. Employees must satisfy a 90-day employment period before using leave. Details the app validator should add: (1) The 90-day wait is a limit the employer may impose, not a requirement. (2) The employer may cap use at 40 hrs / 5 days per year. (3) A front-loaded new hire must have 24 hrs / 3 days available by day 120 of employment. (4) An accrual method other than 1:30 must give at least 24 hrs by day 120 and 40 hrs by day 200. The DLSE letter 'New Wage Legislation 2026' (dated Dec 31, 2025, dir.ca.gov/DLSE/LCO-Letter-to-Employers-New-Laws-2026.pdf) says AB 406 and AB 2499 expanded paid sick leave uses to jury duty, court witness appearances under subpoena, and certain crime-victim situations, and that the poster was updated. The jury and witness uses date from 2025 (AB 2499) and were not created by the letter. Some crime-victim uses took effect Jan 1, 2026, and one victim-services use applies only at 25+ employees.

- **a-iipp-1** [confirmed, threshold 1] Every employer must establish, implement and maintain a written Injury and Illness Prevention Program (8 CCR 3203), though employers with fewer than 10 employees may give safety instructions orally and keep a simplified log of instructions in place of full training records. Value: All employers; under 10 employees = oral communication and simplified records allowed. Effective: Ongoing. Counting: The relaxed rules apply while the employer has fewer than 10 employees.. App: Compliance item at the first hire: upload the written IIPP and name the person responsible. Onboarding task: IIPP safety training. At 10 employees, prompt a switch to full inspection and training records. Employees may request access to the program, which must be given within 5 business days. Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/title8/3203.html
  - Checker note: 8 CCR 3203(a) requires every employer to keep a written IIPP. Employers with fewer than 10 employees get three allowances: they may instruct employees orally ((a)(3) exception); they may keep a log of instructions instead of full training documentation ((b)(2) Exception No. 1); and they may keep inspection records only until the hazard is corrected ((b)(1) exception). Employee access must be given within 5 business days ((a)(8)(B)1). The app action misses one exception. (b) Exception No. 3 covers employers with fewer than 20 employees that are not in a designated high-hazard industry and have a workers' comp experience modification rate of 1.1 or less, or that are in a designated low-hazard industry. For them, written documentation of the program may be limited to three items: the responsible person, scheduled periodic inspections and training. A small financial AI company will likely qualify through 19 employees. So at 10 employees the app should end the oral-instruction and log-only allowances and require inspection records kept for 1 year and per-employee training records (name, dates, type, provider). Under Exception No. 3, it should not require a fuller written program.

(e)(2) requires initial training when the plan is first established, then annually. (d)(1)(B) says to omit personal identifying information from the log. Researched for the pay run prep feature against the primary source named in each entry: statute text on leginfo, the DLSE Enforcement Policies and Interpretations Manual (PDF), the Court of Appeal's opinion PDF, eCFR and the Federal Reserve. One pass only: each claim was read in its primary source, but no independent second check has been done yet. Provider practices are cited as practice, not law. Not legal advice. - **w-payday-holiday** [confirmed, threshold 1] No Labor Code section says what happens when a regular payday falls on a weekend or holiday. The Labor Commissioner's enforcement position (DLSE Manual 7.6 to 7.6.2, relying on Civil Code 7, 9, 10 and 11 and Code of Civil Procedure 12a) lets an employer pay wages due on a Saturday, a Sunday, or a Government Code holiday the employer observes on the next business day. In Parsons v. Estenson Logistics, LLC (Cal. Ct. App., Third Dist., No. C093489, filed Dec. 28, 2022, certified for publication) the court held that Code of Civil Procedure 12a extends Labor Code 204(d)'s 7-calendar-day deadline, when it ends on a Saturday, Sunday or holiday, to the next day that is not one. Paying earlier is always allowed (w-pay-early). Value: Later only to the next business day after a Saturday, Sunday or observed holiday; earlier at any time. Effective: DLSE Manual 7.6 (2002); Parsons (Dec. 28, 2022). Counting: All employees.. App: Pay calendar: a scheduled payday that is not a business day moves to the business day before by default, as payroll providers do; offer 'next business day' as an option that cites this entry. Business days exclude weekends, bank holidays (w-bank-holidays) and company holidays. Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/DLSEManual/dlse_enfcmanual.pdf
  - Checker note: Read DLSE Manual 7.6, 7.6.1 and 7.6.2 in the PDF (manual pages 7-3 and 7-4) and the Parsons opinion PDF (https://www4.courts.ca.gov/opinions/archive/C093489.PDF). The DIR paydays FAQ (Q4, https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_paydays.htm) gives the same next-business-day answer for a holiday the employer closes for, with no citation. Parsons involved a weekly payroll under 204(d); it rests on 12a(b), which reaches deadlines in any code, and it does not discuss the semi-monthly windows in 204(a). It treated the DLSE Manual as persuasive, not binding (footnote 4). Code of Civil Procedure 12a(a) defines a holiday as every Saturday plus the judicial holidays in section 135; per Parsons those include Sundays and most Government Code 6700 holidays, but section 135 (read on leginfo) excludes Columbus Day, Lunar New Year, Diwali, Genocide Remembrance Day and Admission Day, so a 12a-based next-business-day rule does not reach those days even though banks close on Columbus Day. Practice, not law: Gusto says paydays always fall on a weekday and a payday on a weekend or bank holiday moves to the prior business day (https://support.gusto.com/article/100758806100000/add-change-or-remove-a-pay-schedule-for-admins); Justworks rejects bulk-payment pay dates on weekends and bank holidays (https://help.justworks.com/hc/en-us/articles/4414662100507-How-to-Upload-Bulk-Payments). The official reporter citation for Parsons was not opened.
- **w-pay-early** [confirmed, threshold 1] Labor Code 219(a) says nothing in the wage payment article limits paying wages more often, in greater amounts, or in full when or before due, and the article's timing rules cannot be set aside by private agreement. Value: Paying before the due date is always lawful. Effective: Ongoing (amended 2002). Counting: All employees.. App: Pay calendar: the default 'business day before' shift needs no further check, and a semi-monthly payday on the last day of the period it covers is lawful (also within 204(d)). Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=219.
  - Checker note: Read on leginfo (Amended by Stats. 2002, Ch. 40, effective May 16, 2002). Subdivision (b) concerns only the state as employer. DLSE Manual 7.7 to 7.7.1 says the same: paying more often than required is permitted, and the statutory times cannot be set aside by agreement.
- **w-bank-holidays** [confirmed] The Federal Reserve Banks close on 11 holidays a year; a holiday on a Sunday closes them the following Monday, while for a holiday on a Saturday the Reserve Banks stay open the Friday before (only the Board of Governors closes). Direct deposits do not settle on these days, so payroll providers treat them as non-business days. Value: 2026: Jan 1, Jan 19, Feb 16, May 25, Jun 19, Sep 7, Oct 12, Nov 11, Nov 26, Dec 25 (Jul 4 is a Saturday: banks open Fri Jul 3). 2027: Jan 1, Jan 18, Feb 15, May 31, Jul 5 (for Sunday Jul 4), Sep 6, Oct 11, Nov 11, Nov 25 (Jun 19 and Dec 25 are Saturdays: banks open Fri Jun 18 and Fri Dec 24). Effective: K.8 schedule for 2026-2030, last updated July 8, 2026. Counting: Calendar fact, not headcount.. App: A bankHolidays(year) list for paydays and the 'Run payroll by' cutoff, together with weekends and company holidays. Do not reuse federalHolidays() in src/lib/dates.ts for this: it moves Saturday holidays to Friday, which is right for federal offices (5 U.S.C. 6103) but not for banks. Source: https://www.federalreserve.gov/aboutthefed/k8.htm
  - Checker note: Read the K.8 table and footnotes. The Reserve Banks are open, and only the Board is closed, on July 3, 2026, June 18, 2027, December 24, 2027, December 31, 2027 (for New Year's Day 2028) and November 10, 2028; everything closes on July 5, 2027 and November 12, 2029 for Sunday holidays. Gusto's direct deposit page says banks process deposits on business days only and a weekend or holiday payday is paid the business day before (https://support.gusto.com/article/999752211000000/direct-deposit-payment-speeds-in-gusto). OnPay's 'Holiday pay run deadlines' page says ACH does not process on federal bank holidays, so payroll must be run one or two business days early (https://help.onpay.com/hc/en-us/articles/5531458663451-Holiday-pay-run-deadlines). This is an operating fact about the banking system, not a California wage rule.
- **w-salary-first-last-week** [confirmed, threshold 1] Under the federal salary-basis rule (29 CFR 541.602), an exempt employee must receive the full salary for any week in which any work is performed, except that the employer may pay a proportionate part of the salary for the time actually worked in the first and last week of employment; deductions are also allowed for absences of one or more full days for personal reasons, and a deduction may use the hourly or daily equivalent of the weekly salary or any other amount proportional to the time missed. Value: Prorate only the first and last week of employment and allowed full-day absences; every other week with work is paid in full. Effective: Ongoing. Counting: Exempt salaried employees.. App: Pay run estimate for a partial first or last period: annual salary / 260 x weekdays (Monday to Friday, holidays included) on payroll in the period, never more than the regular period amount (annual / periods per year); a whole period on payroll always gets exactly the regular amount. Label it an estimate. Source: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-A/part-541/subpart-G/section-541.602
  - Checker note: Read 541.602(a)(1), (b)(1), (b)(6), (b)(7) and (c) through the eCFR renderer API. (b)(6) adds that someone employed occasionally for a few days and paid a proportion of a weekly salary is not on a salary basis. (b)(7) allows proportional pay in weeks of unpaid FMLA leave, which does not apply below 50 employees; no California rule was found on partial weeks of unpaid CFRA leave. For California, DLSE Manual 51.6 says the Labor Commissioner follows the federal weekly salary standard for enforcement (O.L. 2002.03.01), and the Manual's appendix reproduces the predecessor rule on initial and terminal weeks (former 29 CFR 541.118(c)). No California statute, regulation or DLSE text was found that sets calendar days or working days for prorating a partial semi-monthly period. Practice: Gusto's dismissal payroll uses annual salary / 260 x days worked (https://support.gusto.com/article/100894844100000/run-a-dismissal-payroll-for-admins); Justworks prorates a first or last check by the employee's normal workdays in the period, adjustable for holidays (https://help.justworks.com/hc/en-us/articles/360004527371-Paying-Salaried-Employees).
- **w-exempt-deductions-ca** [confirmed, threshold 1] The Labor Commissioner's enforcement policy on exempt salaries (DLSE Manual 51.6) allows a deduction for a full day or more of absence for personal reasons, computed as monthly salary x 12 / 52 / the usual number of workdays in the week, with no single day costing more than one-fifth of the weekly salary. It allows no deduction for a partial day, for days the employer closes or has no work, or for sickness, unless the deduction follows a bona fide plan that pays for both sickness and disability and the employee has used up that leave. No salary is owed for a week with no work, and the exempt salary floor cannot be prorated for part-time work. Value: Daily deduction = annual / 260 for a 5-day week; full days only. Effective: DLSE opinion letters of 2002 (O.L. 2002.03.01, 2002.04.08, 2002.05.01). Counting: Exempt employees.. App: Pay run: show unpaid full days for exempt staff as a deduction of annual / 260 per day; never deduct partial-day hours or company closure days; flag, and do not deduct, unpaid sick days for exempt staff ('check before docking pay'); a period with no workday worked estimates $0. Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/DLSEManual/dlse_enfcmanual.pdf
  - Checker note: Read Manual 51.6.3 (no proration of the salary floor for part-time work, and no salary owed for a week with no work), the salary basis enforcement policy (weekly standard), 51.6.9 and 51.6.11 (the proration amount and daily method, one-fifth cap), 51.6.14 (employer-caused absences), 51.6.14.3 (full-day personal absences), 51.6.15 (any work in the day bars a deduction) and 51.6.15.2 (sickness). Worked example in 51.6.15.1: $3,000 a month x 12 / 52 = $692.31 a week, / 5 = $138.46 a day. 51.6.5.2 still prints the 2023 salary figure; use w-exempt-salary-2026 for the 2026 floor. Courts treat the Manual as persuasive, not binding (Parsons, footnote 4, citing Tidewater Marine and Alvarado). Under Conley v. PG&E as summarized in 51.6.15, a partial-day absence may still be charged to a vacation bank; that changes the leave balance, not the salary.
- **w-sick-pay-timing** [confirmed, threshold 1] Paid sick leave an employee takes must be paid no later than the payday for the next regular payroll period after it was taken (Labor Code 246(n)). For exempt employees it is paid the same way as other paid leave; for non-exempt employees it is paid at the regular rate for that workweek, or at total wages (without overtime premium) divided by hours worked in the full pay periods of the prior 90 days (246(l)). The employer may set a minimum increment of up to 2 hours (246(k)). Value: Pay by the next regular payday; increment up to 2 hours. Effective: 2024-01-01 (SB 616 text). Counting: All employees.. App: Pay run: list sick hours taken in the period (they are paid this payday or the next); for hourly staff, show sick hours as paid at the regular rate; never treat sick hours as unpaid unless the Admin chose 'Record as unpaid'. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=246
  - Checker note: Read the current section on leginfo (Amended by Stats. 2023, Ch. 309 (SB 616), effective Jan 1, 2024). Subdivision letters in the current text: (k) increment, (l) rate, (n) timing; (r) says (g), (h), (i), (l), (m) and (n) preempt contrary local ordinances. w-psl-wage-statement covers the balance shown with each paycheck under 246(i).
- **w-overtime-next-payday** [confirmed, threshold 1] Wages earned for work beyond the normal work period must be paid no later than the payday for the next regular payroll period, and the hours may then be itemized as corrections on the next pay stub with the dates of the period corrected (Labor Code 204(b)); only those wages may wait. Value: Overtime by the next regular payday. Effective: Ongoing (204 last amended effective 2020-01-01). Counting: Non-exempt employees.. App: Pay run note on hourly and non-exempt rows: overtime from the last period is due this payday; hours come from time tracking or the provider. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=204
  - Checker note: Read 204(a) to (d) on leginfo (Amended by Stats. 2019, Ch. 508 (SB 698), effective Jan 1, 2020). The DIR paydays FAQ (https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/faq_paydays.htm) agrees and adds that straight-time wages may not be delayed this way. w-pay-frequency covers 204(a) and (d).
- **w-payday-notice** [confirmed, threshold 1] Every employer must keep posted, where employees can see it, a notice of the regular paydays and the time and place of payment (Labor Code 207). Violating 204 or 207 is a misdemeanor, and a missing 207 notice is prima facie evidence of a violation (Labor Code 215). Value: Posted notice of paydays, time and place. Effective: Ongoing (207 enacted 1937; 215 amended effective 2009). Counting: All employers with employees.. App: Pay calendar: a printable 'Regular paydays' notice built from the company's schedule, using the same wording as regularPayday on the 2810.5 notice. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=207.
  - Checker note: Read 207 and 215 (https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=215.) on leginfo. The DLSE Manual's discussion of 207 (chapter 7) says no specific form is required and DLSE Form 8 may be used. How a fully remote team should post the notice was not researched.
- **w-late-pay-penalty** [confirmed, threshold 1] Failing to pay wages as Labor Code 204 requires carries a penalty of $100 per employee for each failure in an initial violation, and $200 per employee plus 25% of the amount unlawfully withheld for a subsequent or willful one (Labor Code 210), recovered by the employee or the Labor Commissioner and separate from the final-pay waiting-time penalty (w-waiting-time-penalty). Value: $100, or $200 + 25%, per employee per failure. Effective: 2020-01-01 (AB 673). Counting: Per employee per failure.. App: 'Run payroll by' inbox item: the why line says a missed payday breaks Labor Code 204 and cites this penalty. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=210.
  - Checker note: Read on leginfo (Amended by Stats. 2019, Ch. 716 (AB 673), effective Jan 1, 2020). 210(c): an employee recovers either this statutory penalty or the PAGA civil penalty for the same violation, not both.
- **w-nonexempt-salary-rate** [confirmed, threshold 1] For a non-exempt full-time salaried employee, the regular hourly rate is 1/40 of the weekly salary, and a fixed salary pays only the regular, non-overtime hours whatever the agreement says (Labor Code 515(d)); full-time means 40 hours a week (515(c)). Value: Hourly rate = weekly salary / 40 (weekly = annual / 52, so annual / 2080). Counting: Non-exempt salaried employees.. App: Pay run: for a non-exempt salaried row, show the hourly equivalent (annual / 2080) next to 'overtime hours from your time tracking'. Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=515.
  - Checker note: Read 515(a), (c) and (d)(1) to (2) on leginfo. Weekly = annual / 52 matches DLSE Manual 51.6.11 (monthly x 12 / 52).
